1. Explain why adding and removing liquidity to your exchange does not change
the exchange rate.

The exchange requires every liquidity operation to preserve the current reserve
ratio. If the pool contains E ETH and T tokens, adding dE ETH requires
dT = dE*T/E tokens. Therefore (T+dT)/(E+dE) = T/E. Removal uses the same
proportion in reverse. Integer operations multiply before dividing to minimize
rounding, and min/max exchange-rate bounds reject an operation if the pool price
changed after the client prepared it.

2. Explain your scheme for rewarding liquidity providers and justify the design
decisions you made. How does it satisfy the liquidity rewards requirements in
Section 7?

Each provider owns integer LP shares. Adding liquidity mints shares in the same
proportion as the ETH being added; withdrawing burns the shares required for the
requested ETH amount. Swaps charge the provided 3% fee only in the input asset.
The net input participates in the constant-product calculation, while the fee is
stored in a separate ETH or token fee reserve, so it cannot change the quoted
price or the amount returned to the trader.

When a provider burns s shares out of S total shares, the withdrawal includes
s/S of both current fee reserves. This is the basic reward design requested by
the handout. Rewards remain inside the exchange until withdrawal, so swaps do not
perform expensive transfers to every LP. Checks-effects-interactions ordering and
OpenZeppelin ReentrancyGuard protect the withdrawal transfers.

3. Describe at least one method you used to minimize your exchange contract's
gas usage. Why was this method effective?

The contract stores one share count per provider in a mapping and a single
total-shares value. It never loops over providers during swaps or withdrawals,
so transaction cost does not grow with the number of LPs. Quotes and slippage
bounds are computed through view calls and in the browser before submission;
the state-changing function only verifies the bound. Reserve and fee accounting
also uses a constant number of storage slots and writes per operation.
